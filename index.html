THE MATHEMATICS

Same expected mark. Different risks.

If the error probability is ε and there are n independent questions, the number of mistakes follows a binomial distribution. The expected score is always 1 − ε, but the variance is ε(1 − ε)/n.

The chance of perfection, (1 − ε)n, falls with test length. The chance of a bad score usually decreases on a larger scale, but it has sharp jumps at short lengths. Those jumps are exactly why seven questions wins in the default example.
